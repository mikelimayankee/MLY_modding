# Why summit tilt decreased today with tremor between baseline and eruption steady-state

**Kīlauea summit, 2026-07-11 (HST) — analysis against the record of episodes 1–50**

## TL;DR

You observed the **onset of the precursory phase of episode 51**. Overnight
(July 10 → 11) the north vent went from "rare spatter" to nearly continuous spatter
bursts driven by gas-piston cycles — that is the tremor you saw sitting *between* the
pause baseline and the pre-eruption steady state. At ~4:00 a.m. HST the UWD tiltmeter,
having recovered ~15.3 µrad — essentially 100% of the 15.3 µrad lost in episode 50 —
flattened and then rolled over into deflation. The system has reached its pressure
threshold and is now *leaking*: magma stands at the free surface in the vents and is
venting gas (and small volumes of spatter) instead of storing pressure. In 49 of the
previous 50 episodes this state has resolved within hours to a few days into sustained
fountaining. HVO's July 11 forecast: episode 51 "most likely today or tomorrow,"
sliding to ~July 13 if the deflation is sustained.

## 1. The engine: the inflation–deflation sawtooth

Every episode of this eruption rides the same cycle. During a pause, magma recharges
the shallow Halemaʻumaʻu reservoir and the summit inflates (UWD inflationary tilt).
When the system regains roughly the volume it lost in the previous episode, fountaining
begins; the switch to rapid deflationary tilt *defines* episode onset for HVO. The
episode ends when deflation stops, and tilt snaps back to inflation — a sawtooth
repeated ~50 times since 2024-12-23.

Since episode 5, deflation and re-inflation have been nearly balanced — the basis of
HVO's forecast windows. The recent numbers are remarkably consistent:

| Episode | Deflation during (UWD) | Re-inflation before next onset |
|---|---|---|
| 44 | 17.6 µrad | ~14.9 µrad (→45) |
| 45 | 16.0 µrad | — |
| 46 | ~14 µrad | — |
| 47 | 15.6 µrad | — |
| 48 | 17.1 µrad | 16.8 µrad (→49) |
| 49 | 15.5 µrad | ~16 µrad (→50) |
| 50 | **15.3 µrad** | **~15.3 µrad by 4 a.m. Jul 11 — then flattened** |

The tilt "gas gauge" hit full at almost exactly the moment your deflation began. That
is not a coincidence; it is the threshold.

## 2. The tremor you saw: gas-piston degassing at the threshold

Tremor at Kīlauea's summit is largely *degassing-generated*. That gives three distinct
tremor regimes across the cycle:

1. **Pause baseline** — magma deep in the vents, modest open-vent degassing:
   continuous low-level tremor.
2. **Precursory / threshold state (today)** — magma column standing at the top of the
   vents. A cooled, viscous crust traps gas in a foam layer; the foam buoyantly lifts
   the column (piston up, gas trapped → tremor *stagnates*), then the cap thins,
   ruptures, gas escapes with spattering and the column drains back (piston collapse →
   sharp tremor burst). Net effect on RSAM: elevated, *banded/cyclic* tremor —
   distinctly above baseline, distinctly below fountaining. This week HVO logged the
   bursts at 10–15 min spacing (Jul 6), then 5–10 min with south-vent drainbacks
   (Jul 9), then nearly continuous north-vent spatter after midnight Jul 11.
3. **Eruption steady state** — sustained fountaining, continuous vigorous degassing:
   strong continuous tremor.

Your "signal between the pre-eruption steady state and the normal baseline" is regime
2 — the classic gas-piston band. Chouet & Dawson (2015) characterized these as
"cigar-shaped tremor bursts" at 4–5 Hz accompanying each spatter/drainback event, from
a very-long-period source ~1 km beneath the NE rim of Halemaʻumaʻu.

The same intermediate band has announced episodes since the eruption's first months:
before episode 20 (May 2025), *pulsing* tremor (~3-minute pulses, ~8/hour) began ~9
hours ahead of fountaining; episodes 14, 15 (>100 piston cycles in ~24 h), 24, 34
(>120 events), 41 and 48 all showed the burst-every-5-to-15-minutes cadence before
onset — the same cadence HVO logged this week.

## 3. The tilt decrease: the system venting instead of storing

Three mutually reinforcing reasons the tilt rolled over this morning:

- **Threshold reached.** Recovery hit ~15.3 µrad — the episode-50 deficit — at
  ~4 a.m. There is no more "room" to inflate: at this pressure, magma is pushed to the
  very top of the conduits (HVO: spatter + strong glow "indicates that magma is very
  near the surface"). Continued supply now escapes as gas and spatter rather than
  accruing pressure.
- **Gas pistoning and precursory eruption are mass/pressure withdrawals.** Every
  piston collapse vents accumulated gas; nearly continuous spattering ejects material.
  On a tiltmeter this reads as flattening → small deflation — the same reason tilt
  "sometimes flatten[s] during precursory lava overflows or reverse[s] to deflationary
  tilt" (USGS FAQ) before nearly every prior episode. Notably, the spatter "became
  less vigorous after slow summit deflation began" — the pressure relief is real and
  self-limiting.
- **This pause has a documented habit of deflationary interruptions.** The post-50
  recovery was interrupted July 1–2 and July 6–7 (−0.7 µrad), each time pushing the
  forecast window later (Jul 6–12 → 7–14 → 9–13 → 11–15 → 11–13). Deep-seated
  supply fluctuation is a second, non-precursory contributor this cycle.

## 4. Precedent across episodes 1–50

The intermediate-tremor + tilt-rollover state you caught today preceded essentially
every episode of this eruption ("precursory spatter and overflows... occurred before
nearly every episode" — USGS FAQ). Recent leads from first sustained precursory
activity to fountaining onset:

| Episode | Precursory character | Lead time to fountaining |
|---|---|---|
| 43 | overflows, then immediate onset | ~1 h |
| 44 | ~75 sluggish overflows (south vent) | ~6 days (longest) |
| 45 | overflows from both vents | ~55 h |
| 46 | overflows + spatter | ~31 h |
| 47 | cyclic south-vent overflows | hours |
| 48 | 29 gas-piston overflows (5–10 min long, 15–30 min apart) | ~35 h |
| 49 | low dome fountains | ~5.4 h |
| 50 | south-vent spatter/gas jetting w/ tremor spikes, then overflows | ~2.5 d from first spatter; ~25 h from first overflow |

Three failure modes are on record and worth watching:

- **False starts** (ep 18, Apr 2025): a textbook precursory sequence (glow → rhythmic
  spatter → dome fountains → overflow) began Apr 16, then produced two aborted flows
  and a ~5-day stall before the 10-hour high-fountain phase finally arrived Apr 22.
- **Stall-then-go** (ep 37, Nov 2025): precursory overflows *stopped* and the summit
  briefly deflated on Nov 24; the episode came a day later anyway.
- **Failed run-up** (ep 48, May 2026): a sharp ~1 µrad deflation on May 25 with *no*
  accompanying precursory activity delayed the episode nearly a week. Key
  discriminator: today's deflation is *accompanied by* vigorous shallow activity
  (continuous spatter, gas-piston tremor), which is the precursory signature, not the
  failed-run-up signature.

## 5. Bottom line

The decrease in summit tilt + intermediate tremor on 2026-07-11 is the volcano
standing at its eruption threshold with the valve cracked open: re-inflation is
complete to the episode-50 target, magma is at the vent rims, and gas-piston cycles
are venting pressure as fast as it accrues. Per the 50-episode record, this state is
metastable and short-lived — it ends either in sustained fountaining (most likely
outcome, hours to ~2 days) or, if the deflation deepens without escalating surface
activity, in a delayed onset around July 13. It is a precursor, not an anomaly.
