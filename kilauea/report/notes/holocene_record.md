# Agent sweep notes: Kīlauea late-Holocene eruption-style record

(Recorded from research-agent output, 2026-07-13. Quotes surfaced via web-search
extraction; verify against sources before formal citation.)

## 1. The ~2,500-year cycle (Swanson et al. 2014, Geology)

- "The subaerial eruptive activity at Kīlauea for the past 2500 years can be divided
  into 3 dominantly effusive and 2 dominantly explosive periods, each lasting several
  centuries." Explosive style prevailed ~60% of that time.
- "During dominantly explosive periods, the magma supply rate to the shallow storage
  volume beneath the summit dropped to only a few percent [1–2%] of that during mainly
  effusive periods."
- "Explosive activity apparently correlates with the presence of a caldera
  intersecting the water table."
- Periods: EXPLOSIVE ~200 BC–AD 1000 (Uwekahuna Ash; Kulanaokuaiki Tephra AD 400–1000,
  deposited in the Powers caldera) → EFFUSIVE ~1000–1500 (Powers caldera fills;
  ~1200–1400 Observatory shield overflows the summit) → EXPLOSIVE ~1500–1820
  (Keanakākoʻi) → EFFUSIVE 1823–present.

## 2. ʻAilāʻau eruption (~1410–1470)

~60-year continuous tube-fed summit effusion; 5.2 ± 0.8 km³; covered ~430 km² of Puna
north of the ERZ; created Kazumura Cave; vent near Kīlauea Iki. The "long lava flow"
end-member. Puna inundated only ~3 times in 5,000 yr; intervals ~1,250–2,200 yr.

## 3. Caldera formation ~1470–1510 and 2018 as small analog

- Present caldera (Kaluapele) formed ~1500, at/after the end of ʻAilāʻau — likely
  driven by prolonged reservoir drainage; process poorly understood, possibly over
  centuries.
- 2018: 0.8 km³ collapse, >500 m deepening; "withdrawal of <4% of the stored magma had
  reduced pressure by ~17 MPa, causing the host rock above it to begin to fail
  episodically" (Anderson et al. 2019 Science); >60,000 quakes; incremental piecemeal
  collapse. Largest LERZ eruption + collapse in ≥200 yr. No published volume for the
  ~1500 collapse to compare.

## 4. Keanakākoʻi explosive period (~1500–1820)

300 years of episodic phreatic/phreatomagmatic explosions from a deep caldera whose
floor sat below the water table; two quiescent periods of decades. 1790: pyroclastic
surge killed part of Keōua's army crossing the Kaʻū Desert — deadliest US volcanic
event (estimates ~80 to several hundred). Kaʻū Desert footprints: 1790-era but mostly
NOT Keōua's party; some older.

## 5. 1823–2018 effusive era skeleton

- 1823–1924: 15 eruptions + 11 subsidence events; near-continuous 19th-c. summit lava
  lake (~3 km³ erupted into the caldera by 1840); pause 1894–1907; lake to 1924.
- Feb 1924: lake drains in 2 days → May 1924: ~60 steam explosions over ~10 days
  (groundwater met ~980 °C rock); crater left 115 m deep × 520 m wide.
- 1924–1934: seven small refilling eruptions; then LONGEST recorded quiet ~18 yr
  (1934–1952). 1952: 136-day summit eruption ends the pause.
- 1959 Kīlauea Iki (§7) → Mauna Ulu 1969–74 (1,774 days, ~350M m³, 12 fountaining
  episodes, 540-m fountain Sept 1969) → Puʻuʻōʻō 1983–2018 (35 yr, ~4.4 km³,
  episodes 4–47 high fountains 1983–86) → 2018 LERZ + collapse.

## 6. Summit overflows

Last broad rim overflow = Observatory shield era (~1200–1400, pre-modern-caldera).
Within the modern caldera: 1918–1919 the lava lake overflowed Halemaʻumaʻu and flooded
much of the caldera floor (largest 20th-c. spill, 1919). Current floor ~100 m below
upper caldera floor, rim ~150 m above that (~250 m total); refill since 2020 ≈ 35% of
the 2018 collapse volume. Volume to overtop the outer rim: not published.

## 7. Episodic high-fountaining frequency and supply

Modern instances only: 1959 Kīlauea Iki (17 episodes, 580-m fountain, 36 days),
Mauna Ulu onset 1969 (12 episodes), Puʻuʻōʻō onset 1983–86 (47 episodes), 2024–26
(50+). Roughly once per 1–2 decades in the modern era; geologic-record frequency
unquantified. KEY SUPPLY FACT: Kīlauea Iki was fed by a deep-magma pulse ~5× the
long-term supply rate; Puʻuʻōʻō ran at ~the long-term rate. Episodic fountaining =
pressurization cycles from fresh supply into a constricted shallow system.

## 8. SWRZ history

Eruptions: undocumented late-1700s–early-1800s events incl. two lasting weeks–months
(SIR 2019-5010; Kamakaiʻa/Kealaʻalea Hills activity 1790–1823) · 1823 Keaīwa flow from
the 10-km Great Crack (destroyed a village at Mahuka Bay) · 1868 (with the great Kaʻū
quake) · 1919–20 Mauna Iki (8 months) · Sept 1971 · Dec 1974 · (Jun 2024). Activates
far less often than the ERZ; historically clustered at the explosive→effusive
transition (1790–1823) and summit high-stands (1919–20).
