# Agent sweep notes: Mauna Loa submarine radial vents & shield-stage alkalic lavas

(Recorded from research-agent output, 2026-07-13. Quotes near-verbatim via search
extraction; verify against Wanless et al. 2006 G-Cubed doi:10.1029/2005GC001086 [A]
and Wanless et al. 2006 JVGR 151:141–155 [B] before formal citation.)

## Discovery

- Fall 2002 EM300 multibeam survey of the western submarine flank (4 m vertical /
  25 m horizontal resolution, ~2,100 km² to 4.5 km depth) + four JASON2 ROV dives.
- Before 2002: 33 subaerial radial vents + 1 submarine (the 1877 vent) known. Survey
  found NINE new submarine radial vents (ten total) and three submarine lava flows;
  85 samples collected.
- Submarine radial vents/flows cover 29 km² of seafloor, total ~2×10⁹ m³ (~2 km³).
- Vent ages "range from 128 years B.P. to possibly 47 ka" (eyewitness accounts,
  geologic relations, Mn-Fe coatings, geochemical stratigraphy).
- Morphology split: EIGHT vents erupted DEGASSED lavas forming truncated cones and
  short flows — "despite eruption in water depths sufficient to inhibit sulfur
  degassing" (implies pre-eruption degassing via the shallow/summit system). TWO
  vents erupted UNDEGASSED lavas forming "irregular" cones and longer flows — the
  two alkalic cones.

## The alkalic pair

- Cones: Akihimoana (summit ~1,080 mbsl, ~160 m relief) and Moʻikeha (depth not
  found). "These are the first alkalic lavas to be identified from this volcano" —
  from a volcano "thought to have produced only tholeiitic lavas for at least the
  last ~240,000 years."
- Ages ~15–20 ka (<20 ka) from Mn-coating thickness + sparse sediment cover;
  unspiked K–Ar attempts on all ten flows were UNSUCCESSFUL — no radiometric ages.
- Compositions: Moʻikeha glasses = hawaiite, total alkalies ~5.5 wt%; Akihimoana
  transitional, 3.6–3.8 wt%; both lower CaO/Al₂O₃ + higher TiO₂ than ML tholeiites.
- Same source: "Pb isotope and trace element data indicate that the submarine
  alkalic lavas were produced from the same source generating Mauna Loa tholeiites."

## Petrogenesis / mechanism

- Only one of the two eruptions shows lower-degree partial melting; BOTH are evolved
  with "moderate-pressure (~1.0 GPa) fractionation of pyroxene" — cpx fractionation
  "at ~33 km" (range 0.5–1.5 GPa) driving a tholeiitic parent alkalic.
- "The occurrence of the alkalic lavas only at radial vents may indicate that these
  magmas bypassed the primary conduit, which supplies the summit reservoir with
  predominantly tholeiitic magma"; they "branch off and bypass the main conduit
  system of the volcano and avoid mixing with the voluminous tholeiitic magmas."
- Shield-stage verdict: "Holocene eruptions of Mauna Loa have all produced
  tholeiitic lavas and eruption rates have remained relatively high. Thus, the
  volcano has probably not yet entered the postshield stage" — post-shield alkalics
  are volcano-wide low-degree melts with declining rates; these are localized
  fractionation products.

## Radial vents generally

- ≥33 subaerial radial vents, N/W/SW flanks (majority NW; ~8 on the north flank),
  outside the rift zones. Historic radial eruptions: 1843, 1852 (fissure at 3,920 m,
  ~5 km flow), 1859, 1877, 1935.
- 1859: outbreak at 3,380 m on the NW flank; primarily/exclusively radial; 51 km ʻaʻā
  flow (longest historic Hawaiian flow; 36-km channel) to the sea near Kīholo Bay in
  ~8 days; destroyed Wainanaliʻi and Kīholo villages/fishponds.
- Why radial vents exist (USGS): Mauna Loa is so large that neighboring volcanoes'
  buttressing doesn't confine the north/west upper flanks or the free south-Kona
  flank, so eruptive fissures can radiate outside the rift zones there.

## 1877 Kealakekua Bay submarine eruption

- Summit eruption began Feb 14, 1877; on Feb 24 activity migrated 22 miles to
  offshore fissures — Hawaiʻi's only known submarine eruption in 200 years, ~1 day.
- Steamer Kīlauea entered the bay that morning (account in Hawaiian Gazette, Feb 28,
  1877): "columns of steam and volcanic gas," "blocks of semi-molten lava 60 cm
  across floating on the water," "red, blue, and green lights dancing in the
  waters"; strong quake felt at Keʻei/Kaʻawaloa with 1-m ground cracks running ~5 km
  inland.
- Wanless remapping: vents 1–3 m diameter between 1,050 and 690 m depth WNW of Keʻei
  Point; glassy sheet flow ~1.5 km from vent; pillow mounds on Paʻao cone; the 1877
  eruption "much larger than previously thought, resulting in a 10% increase for
  recent volcanism."

## Isotopes / deep plumbing

- "He-Sr isotopes for the radial vent lavas follow Mauna Loa's evolutionary trend";
  historical lavas ³He/⁴He ≈ 8.5 Rₐ.
- Flows >28 ka: higher ³He/⁴He and ²⁰⁶Pb/²⁰⁴Pb, lower ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr than younger flows;
  trace ratios covary → source variability on ~100 ka timescales.
- Mixing model: plume-type source (Lōʻihi-like) + asthenospheric source (MORB-like
  He, elevated Sr).
