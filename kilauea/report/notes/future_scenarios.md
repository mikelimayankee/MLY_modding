# Agent sweep notes: published "what comes next" thinking (as of Jul 2026)

(Recorded from research-agent output, 2026-07-13. Quotes surfaced via web-search
extraction; verify against sources before formal citation.)

## 1. How the episodic phase ends — HVO's own framing

- Volcano Watch "Hawaiʻi Five-O" (Jul 2026): 50 episodes in 551 days, avg ~11 days
  apart — most fountaining episodes ever recorded at Kīlauea (passes Puʻuʻōʻō's 47).
  Precedents stated: Puʻuʻōʻō 1983 and Mauna Ulu 1969 "both continued after lava
  fountaining episodes ended, with fissures opening nearby that erupted more
  continuous lava flows. Following the end of the 1959 Kīlauea Iki summit eruption,
  magma eventually moved into the East Rift Zone and erupted in a new location."
  "The vents could break down at some point and begin to erupt lava more continuously."
- HVO formal scenario list (Information Statement, Aug 2025, repeated since):
  (1) equilibrium maintained → episodes continue; (2) new shallow pathway → new vent
  near Halemaʻumaʻu or in the UPPER SWRZ (named analog: 1919–20 Maunaiki, 9-month SWRZ
  eruption that grew out of a long-lived Halemaʻumaʻu eruption); (3) migration into
  the ERZ — "the least likely scenario" given vent locations and deformation.
  Episode 30's Aug 2025 dike/new fissure cited as the template for what scenario 2's
  precursors look like (tightly clustered quakes + deformation indicating shallow
  crack formation).
- Ken Hon (Dec 2025): "the most amazing thing I've seen in my life in terms of
  Hawaiian volcanism"; "we can kind of narrow down the possibilities, but we can't
  predict"; HVO "isn't seeing signs of this eruption ending."
- No published probability weights on end-states. No formal probabilistic hazard
  assessment specific to this eruption found.

## 2. Caldera refill numbers

- Through ep 48 (Jun 2026 reference map): average lava surface elevation 1,005 m;
  average new fill thickness ~88 m. Year-one: caldera floor +68 m; new 42-m puʻu on
  the NW rim.
- HVO FAQ: 2018 collapse ~0.8 km³; "eruptions since 2020 have filled only about 35%"
  (date of figure uncertain); much more volume needed to reach the down-dropped block
  level, more still to the caldera rim.
- Refill gets geometrically harder: ~670,000 m³/m of rise at 2021 levels → ~1.4M m³/m
  at higher elevations. "Halemaʻumaʻu is in no danger of filling and overflowing
  anytime soon." No published date forecast for pre-2018 restoration or overflow.

## 3. Explosive-regime question

- Swanson et al. 2014: 2,500 yr = 3 effusive + 2 explosive multi-century periods;
  explosive ~60% of the time; supply during explosive centuries ~1–2% of effusive;
  explosivity correlates with caldera floor intersecting the water table.
- Water table (Keller Well, Volcano Watch Aug 2025): pre-2018 water level 1,600–1,620
  ft below surface; post-2018 1,660–1,670 ft (~240 ft asl). Explosive-era geometry =
  caldera floor ~615 m below rim (2018 deepening approached but did not reach/hold
  that config; 2019–2020 water lake showed the floor DID touch the water table).
- Current HVO framing ("The past is the key to the future at Kīlauea"): today =
  high-supply + refilling caldera = effusive configuration. Long-term verbatim:
  "Eventually the magma supply rate will drop drastically..., the caldera will
  collapse..., and explosive eruptions will resume... a hazardous situation at the
  summit that could last for centuries."
- Tephra-record link (Volcano Watch Apr 2026 "Caldera clues"): high lava fountains
  erupted within Kaluapele ~1500, ~1650, and in the first two decades of the 1800s
  (golden pumice = first high-fountaining in ~300 yr) — "the current eruption style
  is not unprecedented for the volcano's summit region."

## 4. SWRZ / ERZ status

- SWRZ: Jun 3, 2024 eruption (first since Dec 1974; 8.5 h; ~1% of 1974's volume);
  Dec 23, 2024 onset vents on the SW side of Halemaʻumaʻu; upper-SWRZ new vent remains
  a live HVO scenario. 1974 precursors: months of rapid inflation (~30 µrad),
  quakes 2–4/min on the eve.
- ERZ re-pressurization is REAL post-2018: June–July 2024 upper-ERZ intrusion
  (>1,700 quakes, Pauahi–Maunaulu, confirmed by InSAR); Sept 15–20, 2024 Nāpau
  eruption (middle ERZ, 1.8 km of fissures, ~2.9M m³) "resulted from magma intruding
  from the summit into the East Rift Zone"; ongoing MERZ inflation at
  Makaopuhi–Nāpau storage. Volcano Watch (Oct 2024) long-term outcomes: more
  eruptions likely; new pit-crater formation possible ("happened at least 17 times in
  the past 550 years"); intrusions can trigger south-flank quakes ("the flank becomes
  loaded like a spring").
- During the current eruption: ERZ/SWRZ seismicity and deformation LOW (2026 updates).

## 5. Mauna Loa coupling

- Pietruszka et al. 2024 (J. Petrology): ~200-yr lava-chemistry record shows melt from
  a SHARED mantle source is transported alternately to Kīlauea or Mauna Loa on
  decadal timescales; "since 2010... melt from the shared source is now being
  diverted from Kīlauea to Maunaloa for the first time since the mid-20th century."
  (Unreconciled tension with Kīlauea's current high output.)
- Sci Reports 2022: anticorrelated caldera deformation; stress-transfer, not
  hydraulic connection. Gonnermann et al. 2012 Nature Geosci: pore-pressure coupling
  in asthenospheric melt layer; eruption at one can inhibit the other absent a
  supply increase.
- Mauna Loa mid-2026: NORMAL/GREEN, slow re-inflation with pauses, summit slightly
  contracted in June 2026.

## 6. Hazard-relevant misc

- Episode 38 case study: vent geometry changed abruptly → 300-m inclined fountains
  directed south; tephra-buried cracks leave void spaces. Pele's hair to ~30 km;
  episode SO2 to ~50,000 t/d.
- EGU26-9963 (Reiss et al.): plumbing evolution of 2024–26 sequence; episodes entered
  "a stable pattern from January 2025 onwards."
