# Agent sweep notes: Mauna Loa diversion hypothesis (Pietruszka et al. 2024)

(Recorded from research-agent output, 2026-07-13. Primary pages 403 automated fetch;
quotes are as surfaced by search extraction of abstracts/press releases — verify
exact wording against sources, esp. items flagged.)

Paper: Pietruszka, Heaton, Marske, Norman, Robbins, Mershon, Lynn, Downs, Steiner,
Rhodes, Garcia (2024) "Awakening of Maunaloa Linked to Melt Shared from Kīlauea's
Mantle Source," J. Petrology 65(12), egae121, DOI 10.1093/petrology/egae121.

## 1. Evidence base

- Tracers: major elements + trace elements (Sr, La, Ba, Pb) + radiogenic isotopes;
  core systems 206Pb/204Pb and 87Sr/86Sr (plus εNd, Hf). Nb/Y = degree-of-melting
  proxy that first showed the ~2010 shift.
- ~200-year lava-chemistry record from both volcanoes (early 1800s → present).
- "Orthogonal trends between 206Pb/204Pb and 87Sr/86Sr for Kīlauea and Maunaloa lavas
  require at least three distinct sources within the multi-component Hawaiian plume."
  Shared-source composition estimated from the intersection of best-fit lines through
  each volcano's lavas.
- Prehistoric anchor: AD 250–1400 convergence event — Pb, Sr, Nd of BOTH volcanoes
  moved toward an intermediate composition then returned (Marske et al. 2007 EPSL) —
  defines the "shared/S" component.

## 2. Mechanism / geometry

- Three-source model: Kīlauea taps its own (Kea-endmember) source, Mauna Loa its own
  (Loa-endmember), plus a shared intermediate source "likely located between these
  volcanoes." Reframes the plume as "a horizontal gradient of compositional
  heterogeneities rather than a large-scale bilateral asymmetry."
- Connection is in the asthenospheric melting region ">80 km deep" — melt-extraction
  pathways migrate toward one volcano or the other; NOT a shallow shared chamber.
- Switching timescale: decades; mantle-to-surface transit "takes decades," so the
  tool forecasts on multi-decadal horizons only.

## 3. Claimed historical alternations

- Late 19th c.: Mauna Loa active / Kīlauea quiet — shared melt to Mauna Loa; Kīlauea
  chemistry became more "unique"/Kīlauea-endmember.
- Early-to-mid 20th c.: trajectory change — "Kīlauea lavas trending toward the shared
  component and Maunaloa lavas trending away."
- Mid-20th c. → ~2010: Kīlauea highly active (incl. Puʻuʻōʻō) / Mauna Loa near-dormant
  (1950–2022 except 1975/1984); Kīlauea chemistry more Mauna Loa-like.
- ~2010 → present: reversal — "melt from the shared source is now being diverted from
  Kīlauea to Maunaloa for the first time since the mid-20th century"; culminates in
  Mauna Loa 2022 (first eruption in 38 yr).
- Puʻuʻōʻō precursor: hybrid Kīlauea/Mauna Loa source component "progressively tapped
  in greater amounts from 1998 to 2003... explained if melt pathways migrated towards
  Mauna Loa."

## 4. The ~2010 shift

- First seen in Nb/Y; Pb/Sr isotopes trending away from Mauna Loa-like values.
- Pietruszka (press): Kīlauea "is receiving less and less magma from the shared area
  in the mantle plume. Instead, the magma from the shared area... is heading to
  Maunaloa."
- Numeric magnitudes (ΔPb, ΔNb/Y) and sample counts: not in accessible snippets.
- 2018 / 2020–23 / 2024–26 lavas: not confirmed included in egae121 (2024–26
  post-dates it). Separate USGS geochem dataset exists for Dec 2020–Sep 2024
  eruptions (Springer s00445-026-01957-x; USGS 70274271).

## 5. Stated predictions

- "Monitoring of lava chemistry is a potential tool that may be used to forecast the
  eruption rate and frequency of these adjacent volcanoes on a timescale of decades."
- "A future increase in eruptive activity at Maunaloa is likely if the chemistry of
  lava continues to change at Kīlauea."
- No explicit "Kīlauea output will decline" quote found — the diversion applies to
  the SHARED component; Kīlauea retains its own endmember source.

## 6. Critiques / alternatives / related

- Os isotopes (Bharadwaj, Lynn, Pietruszka, Trusdell, Sharma 2026, Chemical Geology
  719): 187Os/188Os on 8 Mauna Loa tholeiites + 3 Kīlauea samples; Mauna Loa nearly
  constant 0.1357±0.0013 over ~200 yr; "the 'shared' mantle source exerts negligible
  control on Mauna Loa Os isotope systematics." Search-layer characterization
  ("effectively refutes the shared mantle source hypothesis") is LIKELY OVERSTATED —
  Pietruszka is a co-author; more plausibly a constraint on the shared component's
  contribution to Mauna Loa. VERIFY against actual abstract.
- Gonnermann et al. 2012 (Nature Geosci): pore-pressure/stress coupling through an
  asthenospheric melt layer; anticorrelation as "competition for the same magma
  supply"; both volcanoes inflated simultaneously in the 2000s.
- Przeor et al. 2022 (Sci Reports): DInSAR/GPS ICA 2003–2010; anticorrelated caldera
  deformation; "excludes a hydraulic connection" — favors elastic stress transfer.
- Poland et al. 2012 (Nature Geosci): mantle-driven supply surge to Kīlauea 2003–2007
  (supply "at least doubled") — deep supply varies on years timescale. NOTE timing
  tension: a surge TO Kīlauea in 2003–07 just before the claimed ~2010 diversion away.
- Wilding et al. 2022 (Science): "magmatic web" — 15-km sill complex at 36–43 km
  depth connecting Kīlauea plumbing toward Mauna Loa's décollement; structural support
  for deep connectivity (at ~40 km, vs Pietruszka's >80 km locus).
- 2022 Mauna Loa trigger paper (Nat Comms 2024): 2-month shallow intrusion; framed as
  shallow-reservoir process. 2022 lava = 38-yr-aged 1984-era reservoir (Rhodes et al.
  2026 Bull Volc: MgO 6.24%, ~145M m³).
- 2024–26 high-output paradox: NO published reconciliation found. Implied escape
  hatches: (a) only the shared component diverts — Kīlauea's own source still feeds
  it; (b) decadal transit lag — current vigor reflects pre-2010 routing.

## 7. Prior work chain

- Pietruszka & Garcia 1999: 200-yr Kīlauea summit record; melting degree halved
  1840–1959 in step with lower supply. Marske 2007 (AD 250–1400 event); Marske 2008
  (Puʻuʻōʻō variations); Pietruszka et al. 2015 EPSL (two summit magma bodies, ≥4
  distinct mantle batches since 1959).
