# Agent sweep notes: filling Kaluapele to overflowing

(Recorded from research-agent output, 2026-07-13. Quotes surfaced via web-search
extraction; verify before formal citation.)

## Rim and floor elevations

- Uēkahuna (old Jaggar/HVO site): highest rim point, >4,000 ft; UWE vault metadata
  1,257 m. Kīlauea summit variously 1,222–1,243 m.
- Volcano House / NE rim: ~4,000 ft (~1,219 m).
- Keanakākoʻi overlook (SE rim): ~3,700 ft (~1,128 m).
- USGS FAQ: upper caldera floor ~1,075 m (3,530 ft); rim "over another 150 m" above it.
- LOWEST rim: the caldera is "breached by lava flows on the southwestern side" (GVP).
  March 1921: "lava from Halemaʻumaʻu and an adjacent cone flowed through a gap in the
  south caldera and into the Kaʻū Desert" — the one historic escape of summit lava
  over/through the rim (all others went via rift zones). Numeric sill elevation of the
  SW breach: not published; 1921 behavior implies ≈ at/just above the upper caldera
  floor (~1,075–1,090 m).

## Pre/post-2018 references

- Pre-2018 Halemaʻumaʻu: ~1 km wide, ~85 m deep (floor ≈ 990 m); 2008–18 lake 20–150 m
  below that floor.
- 2018: floor collapsed ~500 m; base at 518 m elevation; down-dropped block dropped up
  to ~180 m (surface ≈ 975 m; "still about 100 m below the upper caldera floor").
- Jun 2026 (through ep 48): average lava surface 1,005 m; avg thickness 88 m; max
  165 m; flows encroaching nearly the whole down-dropped block, confined to
  Halemaʻumaʻu + SW side of Kaluapele.

## Fill volumes/rates

- 2018 collapse ~0.8 km³; ~35% refilled (FAQ; figure date uncertain).
- 2021 fill-efficiency figures: 670,000 m³ per meter of rise then; ~1.4M m³/m at
  higher levels; no published figures at down-dropped-block/caldera-floor levels;
  no published years-to-overflow estimate. "Halemaʻumaʻu is in no danger of filling
  and overflowing anytime soon."
- Erupted-vs-fill efficiency: ~479M m³ erupted since 2020 vs ~280M m³ of collapse
  volume refilled → ~60% (tephra export, drainback, density, geometry).

## 19th-century precedent

- Post-1500 caldera ~3 km wide, >600 m deep; refilled by lava from 1790 onward to
  within ~120 m of rim by pre-2018 era.
- Black-ledge era: 1825 central pit ~260 m deep; repeated partial drainings (1823,
  1832, 1840 [-150 m during LERZ eruption], 1868, 1886, 1891, 1894, 1916, 1919, 1922,
  1924). 1885/1894 lake overflows preserved on the caldera floor. No "within X ft of
  rim by 1840" figure found.
- 1826: Bishop's Hawaiian guide predicted the filling crater would "discharge itself
  as formerly towards the sea, through some aperture underground" — i.e., rift-zone
  relief valve, which is what history repeatedly did.

## Routing if it overtops

- Ground rises to N/NW (Mauna Loa's flank); NE rim (Volcano House, ~1,219 m) far above
  fill levels. Drainage: SW (Kaʻū Desert, via the breach — 1921, 1971, 1974
  precedents; 1974 flow ran ~12 km SW), S, and SE (Keanakākoʻi ~1,128 m, then Chain of
  Craters corridor — 1971/1974/1982 eruptions south of the rim; Jul 1974 flow 2.1 km
  from fissure SE of Keanakākoʻi).
- Park assets by path: SW — closed south Crater Rim Drive, Kaʻū Desert/Footprints
  trail (trailhead on Hwy 11, 9.2 mi from entrance), Mauna Iki. SE — Keanakākoʻi
  overlook, Devastation Trail/Puʻupuaʻi, Chain of Craters Road (19 mi, descends
  1,100 m to the coast; "parts covered by lava in 41 of the past 53 years"),
  Hilina Pali Road. USGS steepest-descent-lines dataset exists (1983 DEM,
  ScienceBase 57fd072e) but no summit-overflow-specific analysis published.

## Observatory shield / ʻAilāʻau precedent

- ~1200–1400: lava overflowed and buried the (pre-modern) caldera, building the
  Observatory shield; flows went "mostly to the southwest but some went south and
  probably also east and northeast."
- Summit overflows "nearly continuous between approximately AD 1290 and 1470."
- Finale: ʻAilāʻau (~1445, vent near Kīlauea Iki on the NE edge) sent flows down the
  NE flank to the sea (Kaloli Point ocean entry), 5.2±0.8 km³ over ~50 yr — vent
  RELOCATION to the NE margin, not rim overtopping. Then the ~1500 collapse ended
  the overflow era.
