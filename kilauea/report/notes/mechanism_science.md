# Research notes: precursory-signal science (agent sweep, 2026-07-11)

Caveat: quotes were surfaced via web search extraction (direct fetch blocked); treat
as near-verbatim and confirm against source URLs before formal publication.

## 1. Inflation–deflation (DI) cycle between episodes

Magma recharges the shallow summit reservoir during pauses → summit inflates
(inflationary tilt on UWD/Uēkahuna) → repeatable pressure threshold → episode onset →
rapid deflation during fountaining → episode ends when deflation stops; tilt abruptly
reverses to inflation.

- "During the ongoing eruption, Kīlauea summit tiltmeters have shown inflationary tilt
  prior to a lava fountaining episode, as pressure builds beneath the surface. When
  higher lava fountains begin to erupt continuously, the tiltmeters show a switch to
  deflationary tilt... with the switch to deflationary tilt generally defining an
  episode start." — USGS FAQ
- "This pattern of inflation and deflation with every recent lava fountain episode
  creates a saw-tooth pattern in ground tilt records." — USGS FAQ
- "At the end of each episode there is an abrupt change from deflation to inflation as
  the summit begins to repressurize." — Volcano Watch
- EGU26-9963: three phases — "deflation of a shallow and then deeper magma reservoir,
  as well as melt recharge from depth and the dynamics of the shallow reservoir
  controlling the lava fountaining."

## 2. Gas pistoning

Shallow, degassing-driven cyclic rise/fall of the lava free surface. Foam layer traps
gas beneath a viscous cooled crust; buoyant foam pushes the column up like a piston;
when the cap thins, gas releases with spattering and the column drains back.

- "Gas pistoning is the shallow, degassing-driven rise and fall of a lava surface...
  occurs often in the precursory stage of an eruption episode." — Volcano Watch
- Seismic signature: gas trapped → "tremor stagnation" (low RSAM, low SO2); gas release
  and drainback → tremor spike. Chouet & Dawson 2015: "each spattering episode was
  associated with a rapid lava level drop and a conspicuous cigar-shaped tremor burst
  with peak frequencies of 4–5 Hz"; VLP source ~1 km beneath NE flank of Halemaʻumaʻu.
- HVO phrasing in notices: "Low-level seismic tremor continues during the current pause
  with occasional bursts, likely in association with gas piston cycles in the eruptive
  vents."
- Tilt signature: small sawtooth/stepwise deflation offsets; "tilt signals sometimes
  flatten during precursory lava overflows or reverse to deflationary tilt." — USGS FAQ
- Occurs in final hours–days because pistoning requires the magma column standing high
  in the vent — only after re-inflation near threshold.

## 3. Documented final-hours sequence

inflation flattens → slight deflation → tremor rises to intermediate levels →
precursory spatter / small dome fountains / cyclic overflows and drainbacks (gas
pistoning) → hours (up to >24–31 h) of precursory phase, sometimes false starts →
rapid tilt switch to deflation + tremor jump = sustained fountaining onset.

- "Episodes have started with precursory spatter and overflows from one or both vents
  (which occurred before nearly every episode)... lasting hours to days." — USGS FAQ
- Episode 18: "sustained fountaining phase was preceded by almost 31 hours of
  precursory low-level activity that included gas-pistoning events in the north vent
  that produced low lava dome fountains, overflows, and drainbacks."
- Episode 20: onset "accompanied by a rapid change in summit tilt from inflation to
  deflation along with a rapid increase in tremor."
- Episode 50: south-vent spattering/gas-jetting/tremor-spikes from 7:22 p.m. Jun 24 →
  north-vent spatter joined → first precursory overflows ~3:00 a.m. Jun 26 →
  "lava fountaining began a little over 25 hours after the onset of very short (<50 ft)
  precursory lava overflows at 8:50 a.m. HST from the north vent."

## 4. Why intermediate tremor + slight tilt decrease = threshold degassing

Magma standing at the free surface degasses; tremor is largely degassing-generated, so
it sits above pause baseline but below full-fountaining steady state. Pistoning
drainbacks and venting of gas/small lava volumes relieve pressure → inflation flattens
or reverses slightly. The system leaks gas at the threshold until the runaway
transition: "Dense degassed material is pushed out of the vent like a cork from a
champagne bottle, allowing gas-rich magma to rise rapidly and fountain"; fountaining
begins when magma "reaches a critical flow speed that fosters very rapid growth of gas
bubbles." During pauses "molten magma is probably within 150–300 feet (50–100 m) of the
surface within the vent."

## 5. HVO forecast-window method

- "Since episode 5, deflationary and inflationary tilt have been nearly balanced,
  indicating that fountaining episodes occur once the system has regained the volume
  lost during the previous episode... this regularity of the 'target' point is the
  basis for the forecasting windows." — Volcano Watch
- Concrete UWD numbers: episode 50 deflation = 15.3 µrad; recovery as of Jul 10 =
  ~14.1 µrad ("an increase of 2 microradians since yesterday"); example target ~16.6
  µrad before episode 38. Deflationary interruptions during the pause "pushed back the
  forecast window for episode 51."

## 6. Published research status (as of 2026-07-11)

- No dedicated peer-reviewed journal paper on the 2024–26 fountains surfaced yet.
- Strongest: EGU26-9963 (Reiss et al.) seismic+geodetic phases; Volcano Watch
  mechanism articles; "Hawaiʻi Five-O" recap: 50 episodes in 551 days, avg ~11 days
  apart, avg episode ~15 h, max fountain ~1,770 ft (540 m) in episode 43.
- Flag: "stomp-rocket" model = 2018 collapse explosions, NOT these fountains.
- Foundational gas-piston literature: Chouet & Dawson 2015 JGR; Patrick et al. 2016
  EPSL; Johnson et al. 2005; Fee et al. 2010.

## 7. Historical analogs

- Puʻuʻōʻō 1983–86: ~47 episodes every 3–4 weeks, <24 h each; inflation periods with
  increasing shallow short-period seismicity culminating in fountaining + rapid summit
  deflation + LP spike (Colella & Dieterich 2015).
- Kīlauea Iki 1959: 17 episodes; episode 2 "started with low fountains as degassed lava
  that had drained into the vent was erupted out, and eventually, eruption of fresher
  and gassier lava was accompanied by higher lava fountains" — direct analog of the
  degassed-cap → gas-rich fountain transition; fountains to ~580 m (ep 15).
